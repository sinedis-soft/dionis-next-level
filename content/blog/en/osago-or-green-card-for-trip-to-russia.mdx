# OSAGO or Green Card: Which Insurance Do You Need for a Trip to Russia?

To drive a foreign-registered vehicle in Russia, you need third-party liability insurance recognised in the Russian Federation. A standard Green Card issued for travel within the international system does not meet this requirement: Russia has not participated in the Green Card system since 30 June 2023.

If the vehicle owner has no other insurance document issued under an international system recognised in Russia, the practical solution is to take out a Russian OSAGO policy for a foreign-registered vehicle. OSAGO covers the driver’s liability to injured third parties, not damage to or loss of the insured vehicle itself.

<Divider />

## What Is Russian OSAGO?

OSAGO is compulsory third-party liability insurance for vehicle owners. It is required in case the driver causes injury, death, or damage to another person’s property while using the vehicle.

When an insured event occurs, compensation is paid to the injured parties under Russian law and within the applicable statutory terms and insured limits. Having a policy does not mean the insurer must pay compensation in every circumstance: the cause of the accident, the nature of the damage, compliance with the policy terms, and other details of the event all matter.

OSAGO does not insure the owner’s vehicle itself. In particular, the policy does not cover:

- mechanical breakdown;
- theft or unlawful taking of the vehicle;
- vandalism;
- damage to the vehicle if its owner or driver caused the accident.

Protecting the vehicle itself requires separate voluntary cover, such as comprehensive motor insurance, provided such a product is available for the particular vehicle and policyholder.

## What Is a Green Card?

The Green Card is an international motor third-party liability insurance certificate. It is used for travel between countries participating in the Green Card system.

The certificate’s territorial validity cannot be determined from its name alone. Before travelling, check:

- whether the destination country belongs to the Green Card system;
- whether the specific certificate covers that country;
- whether the required territory is excluded in the document itself;
- whether the certificate remains valid throughout the trip;
- whether it can be used for a vehicle registered in the relevant country.

The Green Card is not a universal policy for every international journey and does not replace comprehensive motor insurance. Like OSAGO, it covers third-party liability rather than the vehicle itself.

<Divider />

## How OSAGO Differs from the Green Card

Russian OSAGO and the Green Card serve a similar purpose — both insure the vehicle owner’s third-party liability — but they operate within different legal and territorial systems. They are not two names for the same product, nor are they interchangeable documents.

| Criterion | Russian OSAGO | Green Card |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Compulsory third-party liability insurance for vehicle owners under Russian law | International proof of third-party liability insurance within the Green Card system |
| Primary territory of validity | Russian Federation | Green Card system countries covered by the specific certificate |
| Suitable for driving in Russia | Yes, provided the policy has been issued correctly and remains valid throughout the trip | No. A standard Green Card does not replace insurance recognised in Russia since Russia ceased participating in the system |
| Valid outside Russia | Russian OSAGO alone is not valid outside Russia | Only in the countries covered by the specific certificate |
| Covers the insured vehicle | No | No |
| Replaces comprehensive motor insurance | No | No |
| Available for a foreign-registered vehicle | Yes, for use in Russia | No. A Green Card can only be issued in the country where the vehicle is registered, with an exception for Georgia and Kazakhstan: for vehicles from these countries, Green Cards are issued by Azerbaijani insurers through brokers in Georgia or Kazakhstan |
| Must the entire route be checked? | Yes | Yes |

## What Insurance Does a Foreign-Registered Vehicle Need in Russia?

Russian OSAGO law extends the insurance requirement to owners of vehicles used in Russia. A vehicle registered abroad and entering the Russian Federation must either be covered by an OSAGO contract or insured under an international insurance system recognised by Russian law.

A standard Green Card issued for travel in the countries of the current Green Card system cannot be treated as a substitute for OSAGO in Russia. The membership of the Russian bureau in the system was suspended on 30 June 2023, and previously issued certificates ceased to apply to reciprocal travel from that date.

Before entering Russia, you therefore need to check not merely whether you have a document called a Green Card, but whether your insurance coverage is specifically valid in the Russian Federation. If no other recognised international coverage is in place, a Russian OSAGO policy must be arranged for the foreign-registered vehicle.

The policy must be valid from the date the vehicle is first used in Russia. Before travelling, check the vehicle details, the owner’s or policyholder’s information, the authorised drivers, and the insurance period.

## What Insurance Is Required for Other Countries Along the Route?

One policy does not always cover an entire international journey. The requirements must be checked separately for every country, including transit countries.

For example, a route may require several different solutions:

- for driving in Russia — Russian OSAGO or another form of third-party liability insurance recognised in Russia;
- for a Green Card system country — a valid Green Card certificate that covers the relevant country;
- for a country where the existing certificate is invalid or unrecognised — frontier or local insurance.

Russian OSAGO alone does not replace compulsory third-party liability insurance outside Russia. Likewise, a Green Card does not automatically provide cover in every European country and is not valid in a country merely because it lies along the route to your destination.

A practical route-checking process:

1. List every country you will enter or transit.
2. Check the territorial coverage of each existing policy.
3. Compare your travel dates with the start and expiry dates of the insurance.
4. Confirm the requirements of any country for which your current coverage is insufficient.
5. Arrange any missing policies before beginning the relevant part of the journey.

## Common Mistakes Before a Trip

 [X] Searching for “Green Card for Russia” based on outdated information

Materials published before 30 June 2023 may describe rules that are no longer in force. For Russia, you need to verify that the insurance is current and recognised under Russian law.

 [X] Failing to check the policy’s territorial coverage

The product name alone does not confirm coverage in every country. The territory, insurance period, and terms of the specific document are what matter.

 [X] Treating OSAGO as insurance for your own vehicle

OSAGO protects the financial interest connected with liability to injured third parties. Separate coverage is required to compensate for damage to your own vehicle.

 [X] Insuring only for the destination country

If the vehicle transits several countries, the requirements of each one must be checked separately.

 [X] Providing incorrect details

An incorrect VIN, registration number, owner’s details, period of use, or start date may cause problems when the policy is checked or a claim is settled. Verify all details in the issued document before travelling.

 [X] Driving before the policy takes effect

The issue date and insurance start date may differ. Always rely on the period of validity shown in the policy itself.

 [X] Using proof of payment instead of the policy

A receipt, application screenshot, or payment confirmation is not a substitute for the issued insurance document.

 [X] Buying a document without checking the seller and insurer

Before paying, make sure you understand who the insurer is, who will process the application, and how the issued policy can be verified. A promise of “insurance for every country” without clearly stated territories and terms is reason enough not to buy.

## Key Points

When travelling to Russia, you cannot rely on the familiar Green Card name alone. A foreign-registered vehicle must have third-party liability insurance that is specifically valid and recognised in the Russian Federation. Since 30 June 2023, a standard Green Card has not been a substitute for Russian OSAGO.

If no other international coverage recognised in Russia is in place, the practical solution is normally a Russian OSAGO policy for a foreign-registered vehicle. Insurance for every other country along the route must be checked separately, taking account of transit countries, the territorial validity of each document, and the travel dates.
